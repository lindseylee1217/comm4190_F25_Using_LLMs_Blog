How many people can AI videos fool?

In today’s digital age, the line between reality and fabrication is becoming increasingly blurred, thanks to the rapid advancements AI-generated videos. These videos are widespread, encompassing a wide range of content—from soothing ASMR clips and entertaining animal antics to more sinister applications that include spreading misinformation and creating lifelike videos of political figures. In this blog, I wanted to survey my friends and see whether they would believe my AI video was real.

For this purpose, I used Video AI by invideo, powered by sora AI, which has been taking the general media by storm. I wanted to generate a video that seemed the most innoncent of all–an adorable animal video. I generated it in the form of a tiktok so that it would seem more realistic to my friends.

This was the conversation I had: > Me: Create a realistic tiktok video featuring a dog and cat playing with each other. > > Assistant: Here are two ideas to choose from: > - Option A – “Best Buddies Forever” (30 sec) A warm, cozy, heart-melting short showing a dog and cat playing together in slow motion — light background music, golden lighting, and a cheerful narration about unlikely friendships. > > - Option B – “Battle of the Furballs” (45 sec) Funny, fast-paced, and meme-style — zooms, playful music, and captions as if the dog and cat are having a mock battle, ending in a cuddle. > Me: A, as long as its giving wholesome and fuzzy heart-warming vibes. No narration is needed. > > Assistant: Just to confirm before I generate it: > * Platform: TikTok > * Style: Realistic, cozy, natural lighting, no narration (just music & visuals) > * Duration: ~30 seconds > * Mood: Wholesome, warm, “aww” vibes — dog and cat playing affectionately indoors or in a sunlit garden > > Me: Keep it natural with ambient sounds

Would you like me to include soft background music (like acoustic or piano), or leave it completely natural with ambient sounds (like their playful barks/meows)?

Which one do you prefer, or do you have another tone in mind (e.g., cinematic, ASMR, funny, cute, emotional)?

Then, I shared this video with 7 of my friends and asked them if they thought it was AI while we were sharing tiktok videos together. I worded it in the way below to make the question appear less suspicious: > “Wait is this real or is this AI?”

Survey Results & Reflection

Overall, all 7 of my friends were able to guess that the video was AI-generated. Many of them pointed out that the “video looked fake” because of the textures in the video. Despite being able to accurately identify that it was an AI video, many of them had also exclaimed on the realism of the video and how great the quality was.

If my friends had been any less keen on technology, I think it would have been easy to fool someone into thinking that this was a real video with real animals. Even more, if technology continues to improve at this rapid rate, it’s possible that humans are no longer able to distinguish between AI and real videos anymore.

One thing I also wonder that I hope to investigate more in the future is what source and training materials LLMs use to generate these videos. I’d be curious learn more about how this source material influences the type of product generated.
